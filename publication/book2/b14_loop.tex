\chapter{Loop vs traditional}

\section{The taxonomy (§5d.0)}

A traditional transformer uses each layer's parameters once; a looped (recurrent-depth,
weight-tied) transformer applies the same parameters repeatedly --- effective depth $=$ stored
layers $\times$ loops, parameter count unchanged. Three loop shapes:

\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Shape & What repeats & Who\\
\midrule
model-loop & the whole stack traversed, then repeated & the survey baseline; GPT-6 ``Astra''\\
block-loop & only the middle segment repeats, with input injection & SMELT (2609.01343)\\
layer-loop & each Attention/MoE layer applied recurrently & Loopie (2607.16051)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\medskip
A second taxonomy (2604.17121) cuts by recurrence axis: depth (the looped transformer --- our
subject) vs step (block-recurrent, linear attention, DeltaNet, Mamba, RWKV). The sequence-axis
work (Softpick, LITM theory) sits on the step side --- one more reason the two must not be
conflated.

\section{What changes and what does not}

\textbf{Does not change:} the sequence-axis attention (Loopie's backbone is Qwen3-MoE ---
``its attention mechanism, sparsity pattern, and other architectural details remain the
same''); the training objective. The loop buys depth, not a new attention.

\textbf{Changes:} (1) parameters stay flat; compute scales with the loop count; training a
looped model costs about the same as an untied model of the same executed depth --- you save
parameters, not FLOPs. (2) The KV cache grows per stack: $T$ separate KV sets per position
(first-order at 1.5 BPW $\times$ 4M window; S4's shared-KV cuts $15 \to 4$). (3) Backprop runs
through every application.

\section{The Loopie recipe}

Jointly choose stored width, stored depth and recurrent depth to maximise performance under a
fixed pre-training compute budget --- loops as a substitute for parameters, chosen jointly, not
a direct swap.

\section{The measured bar}

LoopQ (2605.16343): W4A4 $+67.8\%$ five-task accuracy, $+75.2\%$ perplexity vs the strongest
static PTQ. LoopQuant (2610.16368): W4A4 beats all W4A8 baselines on Ouro-2.6B, $-65.3\%$
memory, $+3.4\%$ storage, $1.7\times$ decode. 2609.30820: feedback exposure + calibration
blindness --- Hessian accumulation across recurrence steps wins 9/9 and recovers bf16 on
Huginn-3.5B. The intersection thesis: the gap at 1.5 BPW on a looped model is unsolved ---
Track B's target.

\section{The design consequences for this engine}

The looped seam is where the ladder meets the loop: the loop-entry adapter gets an identity
path (S5); $G$ enters the quantisation test set from day one (S6); the KV policy decision
(\#22) recommends shared-KV; the default loop ladder recommends $T = 2$ measured to 4 (\#17);
the routing seam must shrink work, not just depth (G7). Every fork carries its ledger id; the
operator stamps the rulings.
